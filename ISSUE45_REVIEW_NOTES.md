# Issue #45 Review Notes: AI Appraisals for Prose-Level Thesis Weaknesses

Working notes for a future PR body. These are review findings, not final edits.

## Operating premise

The editorial posture should treat the thesis as correct: Elden Ring is The Large Glass, not merely influenced by it or thematically adjacent to it. The task is not to weaken the claim. The task is to make the truth harder to evade and easier for readers to recognize.

Public-facing prose can still be calibrated for persuasion: avoid substituting insistence for demonstration, keep the central claim strong, and make the evidentiary structure legible.

## 1. Reduce overuse of “literal/literally”

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit, plus selected TL;DR/supporting pages.

The site needs “literal” as a core distinction, but repeated uses of “literal,” “literally,” and “made literal” can start to read as emphasis rather than precision. This risks making the prose sound more insistent than authoritative.

Recommended approach:

- Keep “literal” where it distinguishes the thesis from metaphor, influence, or thematic resemblance.
- Cut or replace weaker repetitions where the word only intensifies the sentence.
- Use more precise alternatives where appropriate: “instantiated,” “spatialized,” “embodied,” “given playable form,” “rendered as world-physics,” “made mechanical,” or simply omit the adverb.

The goal is not to hedge the thesis. It is to preserve the force of “literal” by using it less casually.

## 2. Reverse the mapping language

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The current formulation can sound as though Duchamp’s mechanisms are being mapped onto Elden Ring. The direction should be reversed: Elden Ring maps onto, resolves into, or discloses itself as The Large Glass.

The Large Glass is the key. Elden Ring is the object being decoded.

Possible framing:

> Once The Large Glass is recognized as the key, Elden Ring’s characters, items, locations, rules, and cycles resolve into Duchamp’s apparatus.

Or stronger:

> Elden Ring is not decorated with references to The Large Glass. Its characters, items, locations, rules, and cycles map onto Duchamp’s apparatus so completely that the game resolves as the Glass in playable form.

This preserves certainty while fixing the direction of discovery.

## 3. Foreground the observer / notes / meaning-making parallel early

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

A major allegorical parallel should appear earlier: both The Large Glass and Elden Ring require the observer/player to create meaning by assembling fragments.

Duchamp did not intend The Large Glass to be understood as a purely visual object. The Green Box and White Box notes are part of the apparatus, not external explanation. The viewer has to read, compare, decode, and become an initiate. Meaning is produced through the viewer’s interpretive act.

Miyazaki’s games work the same way, but scaled into a video game and community form. Elden Ring’s item descriptions, NPC fragments, environmental clues, cut-content traces, and public influences function as the game’s “notes.” The player studies fragments and participates in constructing meaning.

The crucial expansion is that Miyazaki turns Duchamp’s often solitary initiate model into a collaborative activity: millions of players grinding through the same machine in “jolly cooperation.”

This point answers several likely AI-review objections:

- The lore is incomplete because incompletion is the method.
- The player/reader is doing interpretive work because observer participation is part of the work.
- Item descriptions are not supplementary lore. They are Elden Ring’s Green Box.
- The hiddenness is not incidental. It is the reception machinery of the artwork.

Suggested early paragraph direction:

> The deepest correspondence is methodological. Duchamp did not make The Large Glass as an image to be understood at a glance. He made an image-text apparatus: the glass, the Green Box, and later the White Box, with the notes functioning as part of the work rather than as external explanation. The viewer has to become an initiate, assembling the machine from fragments. Elden Ring uses the same method. Its item descriptions, NPC fragments, environmental clues, cut-content traces, and public influences are not supplementary lore. They are the game’s Green Box. The difference is scale: Duchamp’s initiate was often alone with the notes; Miyazaki turns initiation into a collaborative act, millions of players grinding through the same machine in jolly cooperation.

Exact Duchamp wording around “only an initiate” should be verified before quotation marks are used.

## 4. Clarify game-side evidence posture

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The game-side claims should be described as built to survive specialist scrutiny, without invoking private validators or implying public endorsement by people who may not want to be named as participants.

Preferred formulation:

> The game-side claims are meant to survive specialist scrutiny. They rest on item text, mechanics, visual design, world structure, and the internal logic of the endings, not on vibes, lore gossip, or loose resemblance.

This is stronger than an unnamed appeal to authority and keeps the evidence in the work itself.

For the Duchamp side, cite Rhonda Shearer through her published scholarship and existing site context, not as a claimed public endorser.

## 5. Clarify that coincidence is the less plausible explanation

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The skeptical default will be “maybe this is coincidence.” The thesis should state that, at this density and specificity, coincidence is not the conservative explanation. It is the more extravagant one.

Possible language:

> Any single correspondence can be dismissed. The machine cannot. Coincidence can explain an echo; it cannot economically explain a system.

Or:

> The correspondences are not persuasive because any one of them is impossible to dismiss. They are persuasive because coincidence has to explain all of them at once.

This strengthens the argument without hedging.

## 6. Distinguish load-bearing correspondences from supporting and speculative ones

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit, possibly Master List follow-up.

The thesis includes several kinds of evidence. A skeptical reader can attack a speculative echo and pretend they have damaged the whole argument. The prose should prevent this by stating that not every correspondence carries the same load.

Suggested categories:

1. **Load-bearing correspondences**: Marika/Bride, Tarnished/Bachelors, Elden Ring and Great Runes/Chocolate Grinder, Grace/Love Gasoline, Shattering/broken Glass, Radahn/Juggler of Gravity, Three Mending Runes/Three Nets.
2. **Supporting correspondences**: Roundtable Hold/Paradise of Bachelors, Golden Bough magic system, Daisugi cosmology, Tokyo reproduction/Miyazaki access, readymades/hidden-in-plain-sight method.
3. **Speculative or open correspondences**: Romina/Butterfly Pump, some DLC correspondences, cut-content dependent claims, some extended literary parallels.

Possible language:

> Not every correspondence carries the same load. Some are structural: without them, the thesis would fail. Some are circumstantial: they explain why this structure could have reached Miyazaki and why it appears in the form it does. Some are speculative: useful as trails, but not required for the case. The argument rests on the first group.

This is not hedging. It makes the thesis harder to strawman.

## 7. Rework the TL;DR overclaiming sentence

**Classification:** TL;DR document edit.

The TL;DR phrase “The entirety of The Large Glass is transcribed into the game, down to the smallest details” is too brittle. It gives skeptical readers an easy target.

Possible replacement:

> Elden Ring gives The Large Glass playable form: its roles become characters, its mechanisms become systems, its notes become item descriptions, and its cycle of frustrated desire becomes the player’s own loop through the Lands Between.

Or:

> The Large Glass is transcribed into the game as an apparatus: roles, mechanisms, visual forms, rules, cycles, and acts of interpretation.

The revision should remain certain, just less vulnerable to nitpicking.

## 8. Add a bridge after “This is that”

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The line “This is that” is strong and should stay. It may benefit from one clarifying sentence after it:

> Not a secret object inside the game, but the secret structure of the game.

Or:

> Not a hidden room, not a boss, not a datamined mechanic: a hidden interpretive structure, visible only when the game is read through the artwork whose method it has inherited.

This helps readers understand what kind of secret is being claimed.

## 9. Defer Copy Prompt / skill wording changes

**Classification:** AX / agent-experience follow-up.

The prompt language that tells LLMs not to hedge the claim is useful but could sound defensive to reviewers. Because issue #45 is prose/thesis first, and prompt/agent-pathway changes are borderline AX, this should be logged for later rather than mixed into the first prose pass.

## 10. Add more images throughout the thesis and supporting pages

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit if using existing evidence figures; AX/content follow-up if new assets, galleries, zoom tools, or comparison components are needed.

The thesis is highly visual and mechanical. Readers are asked to recognize correspondences between The Large Glass, Duchamp’s components and diagrams, Elden Ring symbols, item icons, boss designs, geography, runes, and environmental structures. More images would make the argument easier to see and harder to dismiss.

Priority image targets:

1. Opening / early thesis: one strong comparison figure establishing this as a visual-mechanical claim.
2. Chocolate Grinder / Great Runes: Duchamp’s grinder, Elden Ring logo, Godrick/Morgott anchor runes, roller runes, and annotated comparisons if available.
3. Bride / Marika / Erdtree / Elden Beast: upper apparatus and imprisonment imagery.
4. Tarnished / Malic Moulds: class/type correspondence.
5. Juggler of Gravity / Radahn: Duchamp diagram/source image and Radahn gravity imagery.
6. Green Box / item descriptions: Green Box or note images beside representative Elden Ring item-description screenshots.
7. DLC / Miquella / unfinished mechanisms: Miquella crosses, relevant Green Box diagrams, final boss imagery.

Suggested PR note:

> The thesis needs more visual evidence figures. The argument is fundamentally comparative and mechanical, but several major correspondences are currently carried by prose alone. Prioritize side-by-side or captioned figures for the Chocolate Grinder/Great Runes, Bride/Marika, Malic Moulds/Tarnished, Green Box/item descriptions, and Radahn/Juggler of Gravity. This can be a follow-up content pass unless suitable assets are already in the repo.

# Additional skeptical review passes

These notes come from rereading the production Living Thesis, TL;DR, and Master List as a skeptical reviewer rather than as a collaborator already inside the thesis.

## Skeptical pass A: hostile but fair art-history reader

### 11. The Shearer/readymades detour is powerful but could be better signposted

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The readymades section is one of the strongest parts of the thesis because it gives a historical precedent for the kind of hidden-in-plain-sight deception the argument claims Elden Ring performs. However, a skeptical art-history reader may initially experience it as a long detour away from Elden Ring unless the section’s function is announced more explicitly before it begins.

The section already states why Shearer matters, but the transition could be sharpened before the detour starts:

> Before the game can be read correctly, Duchamp’s method has to be corrected. The readymades are not a side topic; they are the precedent for the kind of concealed construction Elden Ring repeats.

This would tell the reader why they are being asked to spend time on readymades before the game evidence resumes.

### 12. Claims about Duchamp lying should be separated from what is needed for the argument

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The thesis is strongest when it distinguishes necessary claims from authorial suspicion. The text sometimes moves from Shearer-supported forensic correction into broader claims like Duchamp being a “compulsive liar” or likely staging the Glass break. Those may be true and rhetorically vivid, but a skeptical reader may treat them as overreach.

Recommendation: keep the suspicion, but more clearly mark it as non-load-bearing. The argument does not require proving Duchamp intentionally broke the Glass. It only requires that the break was incorporated into the work and became part of its meaning.

Possible note:

> The intentional-break hypothesis is not required for the correspondence. What matters is that Duchamp accepted the fracture, rebuilt around it, and made brokenness part of the work’s final state.

This keeps the stronger posture without giving skeptics an unnecessary target.

## Skeptical pass B: Elden Ring lore specialist

### 13. The Radahn/DLC claim needs extra care because it is a fandom pressure point

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit, possibly Critiques response later.

The claim that Radahn’s DLC role completes the Glass is strong, but it sits exactly where Elden Ring fandom skepticism is likely to be highest. The text correctly notes that fans were baffled by Radahn’s return, but a skeptical lore reader may need more direct handling of why “fan service/franchise padding” fails as an explanation.

Recommendation: add a compact contrast:

> The fan-service explanation accounts for Radahn’s popularity. It does not account for why the specific popular character returned in the exact role Duchamp left unfinished: the Juggler of Gravity entering the Boxing Match at the end of the completed apparatus.

This would make the argument harder to wave away.

### 14. Cut-content-dependent claims should be explicitly labeled as such

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The Flower Crucible/Romina/Butterfly Pump material already says it cannot be proven conclusively, which is good. The skeptical pass suggests making this practice consistent anywhere cut content is used. Cut content is useful evidence, but it is a different class from shipped item text, world geometry, boss mechanics, or ending structure.

Recommendation: when a claim depends on cut content, label it as “cut-content support” or “speculative/supporting,” not as a load-bearing proof.

This ties into the earlier need to distinguish load-bearing/supporting/speculative evidence.

## Skeptical pass C: rhetoric/prose reviewer

### 15. The strongest sentences sometimes compete with each other

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The Living Thesis has many excellent thesis sentences: “This is that,” “The secret was never in the code,” “Sometimes when you know what you are looking at, everything becomes clear,” “Video games were the only medium...,” etc. The skeptical prose read suggests that some of these could be made more effective by reducing nearby emphatic repetition.

Recommendation: preserve the best aphoristic lines and cut weaker intensifiers around them. This overlaps with reducing “literal/literally.” The goal is to let the best sentences land.

### 16. Some editorial notes are valuable but may interrupt the argument’s authority

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The editor’s notes provide personality and intellectual honesty, especially around Duchamp’s breakage and Nine Shots. But a skeptical reviewer may read too many as side-channel argumentation. The notes should remain where they clarify evidentiary status; they should be shortened or integrated where they mainly vent suspicion.

Recommended principle:

- Keep editor’s notes that classify uncertainty.
- Shorten editor’s notes that give skeptics a new fight unrelated to the main correspondence.

## Skeptical pass D: structure/navigation reviewer

### 17. Master List should distinguish proof weight, not only topics

**Classification:** Master List document edit / possible follow-up.

The Master List is useful as a catalog, but it currently presents heterogeneous correspondences in a flat list. A skeptical reader landing there may not know which entries are foundational and which are supporting, speculative, or side-theory.

Recommendation: add a short legend or tags:

- **Core / load-bearing**
- **Strong support**
- **Speculative / open**
- **Contextual / literary**

This does not weaken the claim. It prevents skeptical readers from treating every entry as equally necessary and then attacking the most speculative item.

### 18. Master List needs more links back into full explanations

**Classification:** Master List document edit / AX-content follow-up.

As a skeptic, the Master List makes many compressed claims that are persuasive only if the reader has already read the Living Thesis. It would be stronger with links from major bullets to the full explanation sections or relevant pages.

Priority links:

## Skeptical pass E: proof-standard reviewer

### 19. The thesis should explicitly define “self-proving”

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

Near the end, the thesis says:

> The puzzle is self-proving in the cleanest possible way. You can only discover that Elden Ring is art by first treating it as art.

This is a strong idea, but “self-proving” can trigger skepticism because it can sound circular. The point is better than the phrase may suggest: the discovery selects for the mode of attention required to find it.

Recommendation: either define “self-proving” immediately or rephrase:

> The puzzle verifies its own required method: only the reader willing to treat the game as art will perform the kind of looking that reveals the structure.

This preserves the claim while avoiding the appearance of circular reasoning.

### 20. “Video games were the only medium...” is excellent and should be prepared earlier

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit.

The line “Video games were the only medium in which what Duchamp started could be completed” is one of the strongest in the thesis. A skeptical reader would benefit from having this idea seeded earlier: interactivity is not an add-on to the mapping, but the medium that completes the Bachelor Machine.

This connects directly to the observer-created meaning point and the multiplayer/jolly cooperation point.

### 21. The “every video game is a bachelor machine” claim is compelling but enormous

**Classification:** Living Thesis / possible Critiques response.

The final section broadens from Elden Ring to all video games. This is exciting but large enough that a skeptical reader may feel a second thesis has begun. It should either be framed explicitly as the final implication of the discovery or given a future expanded essay.

Possible bridge:

> This is no longer the evidence for Elden Ring being The Large Glass. It is the implication of that evidence: once the Glass is playable, the whole medium begins to look like a bachelor-machine form.

This would help separate proof from consequence.

## Skeptical pass F: source/citation reader

**Classification:** Living Thesis / document edit, images/source follow-up.

The thesis cites many sources, but some highly specific claims would be stronger with visible citations or source anchors close by, especially:

### 23. Image captions should do argumentative work
